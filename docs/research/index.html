Research

Building generalizable AI systems that reason, learn, and act in the physical world.

I am a senior undergraduate researcher at Southeast University, working on multimodal foundation models, agentic AI, and embodied intelligence. My research focuses on how AI systems acquire, organize, and generalize knowledge, reason over complex environments, and translate reasoning into reliable real-world actions.

Foundation Models

I study post-training, knowledge management, model architectures, privacy-preserving adaptation, and generalization in multimodal large language models. My work includes reversible knowledge externalization, modular memory, gradient interference mitigation, and efficient fine-tuning.

  • Multimodal Large Language Models
  • Post-Training and Model Adaptation
  • Knowledge Management and Model Memory
  • Mixture-of-Experts
  • Model Generalization
  • Trustworthy and Privacy-Preserving AI

Agentic AI

I investigate how intelligent agents reason, acquire reusable skills, interact with tools, and improve through experience. I am particularly interested in developing rigorous benchmarks and evaluation frameworks that reveal the true capabilities and limitations of foundation-model agents beyond conventional task success metrics.

  • Agent Benchmarking and Evaluation
  • Agent Harness and Tool Use
  • Long-Horizon Reasoning
  • Procedural Memory and Skill Learning
  • Agent Self-Evolution
  • Program-Level Diagnosis

Embodied AI

I explore vision-language-action models, spatial reasoning, language-action alignment, world models, and closed-loop decision-making. Autonomous driving serves as an important testbed for studying generalization, reliable instruction following, and the interaction between perception, reasoning, and action.

  • Vision-Language-Action Models
  • Language-Action Alignment
  • Spatial Intelligence
  • World Models
  • Autonomous Driving
  • Robotics and Physical-World Interaction

About Me

I am a senior undergraduate student at the School of Instrument Science and Engineering, Southeast University, pursuing a B.Eng. in Intelligent Sensing Engineering. My research lies at the intersection of multimodal foundation models, agentic AI, and embodied intelligence.

I am particularly interested in building generalizable AI systems that can acquire and manage knowledge, perform long-horizon reasoning, interact with complex environments, and translate high-level intentions into reliable actions.

My research spans three interconnected directions:

Multimodal Foundation Models. I study post-training, knowledge management, model architectures, privacy-preserving adaptation, and generalization in multimodal large language models. My work includes reversible knowledge externalization, modular memory, gradient interference mitigation, and efficient fine-tuning.

Agentic AI and Evaluation. I investigate how intelligent agents reason, acquire reusable skills, interact with tools, and improve through experience. I am particularly interested in developing rigorous benchmarks and evaluation frameworks that reveal the true capabilities and limitations of foundation-model agents beyond conventional task success metrics.

Embodied Intelligence. I explore vision-language-action models, spatial reasoning, language-action alignment, world models, and closed-loop decision-making. Autonomous driving serves as an important testbed for studying generalization, reliable instruction following, and the interaction between perception, reasoning, and action.

Previously, I conducted research at the Foundation Model Team of Li Auto, the Institute for AI Industry Research (AIR) at Tsinghua University, and China Automotive Innovation Corporation (CAIC). These experiences have allowed me to work across academic research, industrial-scale data curation, multimodal model development, and real-world engineering systems.

Beyond AI research, I have hands-on experience in robotics, electronic circuit and PCB design, mechanical engineering, embedded development, simulation, and system integration. I aim to bridge foundation-model intelligence with the physical world through both rigorous research and practical engineering.

My long-term goal is to develop intelligent systems that can autonomously understand, reason about, interact with, and create within the physical world.

01

Language2Drive: Do Vision-Language-Action Models Really Follow the Instructions in Autonomous Driving?

  • First Author
  • Project Lead

Released benchmark · arXiv preprint

Language2Drive is a benchmark for whether a natural-language instruction causally determines the behavior of a driving vision-language-action model. Its paired counterfactual unit (PCU) holds the initial driving scene fixed and changes only the instruction, so a successful drive can be distinguished from actually following both feasible requests.

Across the six principal CARLA systems, 93.2% of successful individual instruction executions lack a successful counterpart under an alternative feasible instruction in the same scene. That figure is the share of successful one-sided executions, not an overall failure rate of the models.

  • Six purpose-built CARLA maps
  • Four instruction-following scopes
  • Paired counterfactual evaluation
  • Closed-loop and open-loop tracks across CARLA, reconstructed scenes, and recorded driving
  • Reported CARLA comparison: 64 PCUs and 384 core rollouts per system
  • Authors affiliated with 19 institutions

02

Knowledge Externalization: Reversible Unlearning and Modular Retrieval in Multimodal Large Language Models

  • Co-First Author

ICLR 2026

Knowledge Externalization transfers targeted parametric knowledge into modular, learnable memory tokens. The same tokens support reversible unlearning, controllable recovery, and compositional retrieval.

Dual-Stream Memory fine-tuning optimizes forgetting, recovery, and retention of general capability together. The work also studies gradient interference when several concepts are externalized, and introduces MEXBench to evaluate modular knowledge externalization.

  • Reversible unlearning and modular knowledge recovery
  • Multimodal post-training
  • Knowledge disentanglement and compositional memory
  • Gradient interference mitigation

03

Agents' Last Exam: A Benchmark for Real-World Product Creation

Also called the Idea2Product Benchmark.

  • Initiator
  • Project Lead
  • Ongoing Research

Ongoing research

Agents' Last Exam is an ongoing benchmark for the end-to-end ability of foundation-model agents to create functional physical products from natural-language requirements.

It is being designed to ask whether an agent can coordinate long-horizon reasoning, task decomposition, professional tool use, cross-domain engineering, experimentation, and system integration, and then deliver a physical artifact that someone else can check.

The design target is 24 product blueprints and 48 task instances across six engineering domains: circuit design, PCB development, mechanical CAD, embedded software, simulation, hardware-in-the-loop testing, and physical system integration. The benchmark and the task set have not been released, and no model evaluation results are reported here.

  1. 1Human Requirements
  2. 2Agent Planning and Tool Use
  3. 3CAD / EDA / Embedded Development
  4. 4Fabrication and Integration
  5. 5Independent Physical Verification
  • From human intent to functional physical products
  • Independently checkable functional requirements
  • Physical feedback and capability composition
  • Can agents turn an open requirement into a working physical artifact?
  • Can they compose engineering work across professional domains?
  • Can they experiment and diagnose real-world failures?
  • How should physical task completion be checked independently?

This project is in progress. There is no paper, code release, dataset, or leaderboard to link yet.

  • First Author

arXiv preprint

SAMoE-VLA studies the limits of token-level mixture-of-experts routing when a model has to generate continuous driving actions. It routes experts from structured visual scene features and merges them with differentiable parameter-space fusion.

Dual-Priv Pruning : Efficient Differential Private Fine-Tuning in Multimodal Large Language Models

  • Co-First Author

arXiv preprint

Dual-Priv Pruning is a privacy-preserving fine-tuning framework that prunes both visual tokens and gradient updates. The aim is to reduce computation and limit the utility loss that differential privacy noise causes in a multimodal model.

ICLR 2026

Knowledge Externalization: Reversible Unlearning and Modular Retrieval in Multimodal Large Language Models

Knowledge Externalization moves targeted parametric knowledge into modular memory tokens so a multimodal model can forget, recover, and compose that knowledge. Dual-Stream Memory fine-tuning and MEXBench study this post-training setting, including gradient interference when several concepts are externalized together.

ICPR 2026

MambaBEV: A BEV-Based 3D Detection Model with Mamba2

MambaBEV is a BEV 3D detector with a Mamba2 temporal fusion module for global context. On nuScenes, the paper reports 51.7% NDS and 42.7% mAP for MambaBEV-base.

ICPR 2026

Hybrid Guided Variational Autoencoder for Visual Place Recognition

This paper studies a hybrid guided variational autoencoder for visual place recognition. The authors release the code and the Aachen-Indoor-VPR event and RGB dataset with the paper.

arXiv 2026

Language2Drive: Do Vision-Language-Action Models Really Follow the Instructions in Autonomous Driving?

Language2Drive tests whether a natural-language instruction changes what a driving vision-language-action model does. Its paired counterfactual unit keeps the scene fixed and changes only the instruction. Across the six principal CARLA systems, 93.2% of successful individual instruction executions have no successful counterpart under the alternative feasible instruction.

arXiv 2026

World4Scorer: Outcome-Grounded World Modeling for Autonomous Driving

World4Scorer builds a driving scorer as a trajectory-conditioned predictor. Simulator outcomes supervise every candidate plan, and the observed future of the executed trajectory anchors the shared predictor during training. I am a co-author.

arXiv 2026

MobileWAM: Bridging World Action Models to Mobile Manipulation with Chain-of-Foresight

MobileWAM adapts a video world-action model to mobile manipulation. The action expert mixes shared, locomotion, and manipulation pathways, and Chain-of-Foresight adds training-only future-latent supervision. The manuscript reports a 73.0% mean success rate on ManiSkill-HAB and transfer to a real ARX Lift2 robot. Code has not been released.

arXiv 2026

SAMoE-VLA: A Scene Adaptive Mixture-of-Experts Vision-Language-Action Model for Autonomous Driving

SAMoE-VLA studies scene-level mixture-of-experts routing for continuous driving actions, using bird's-eye-view features rather than token-level expert selection. The public repository currently contains the project overview and figures, not a full training implementation.

arXiv 2025

Dual-Priv Pruning : Efficient Differential Private Fine-Tuning in Multimodal Large Language Models

Dual-Priv Pruning combines visual-token pruning and gradient-update pruning for differentially private fine-tuning of multimodal large language models, together with low-rank adaptation and Rényi differential privacy accounting.

arXiv 2026

SparseOccVLA: Bridging Occupancy and Vision-Language Models via Sparse Queries for Unified 4D Scene Understanding and Planning

SparseOccVLA connects semantic occupancy and vision-language models through sparse queries for 4D scene understanding and planning. I am a co-author on the paper.
